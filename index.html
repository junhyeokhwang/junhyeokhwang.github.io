About

I study how thick lithium-ion battery electrodes actually work from the inside, and turn that understanding into rules for designing and manufacturing them.

I am a Ph.D. student at DGIST in the Battery Design & Processing Lab. My current work is on depth-gradient thick cathodes: controlling how particles pack layer by layer so that ions can still reach the bottom of a high-loading electrode at high rates. I combine 3D microstructure imaging, impedance analysis and image-based simulation to connect electrode structure to performance.

Before DGIST, I completed my B.S. and M.S. at Pukyong National University with Prof. Pilgun Oh, where I worked on Ni-rich cathode materials, solvent-free (dry) and drying-free thick electrodes, and upcycling of spent cathodes.

Research

Designing electrodes that stay uniform when they get thick

Gradient design

Layer-wise packing control of thick cathodes, guided by particle-packing models, to lower ionic resistance at the same loading.

Seeing inside

FIB-SEM 3D reconstruction, transmission-line impedance and depth-resolved analysis to measure where reactions happen through the thickness.

Process to cell

Wet slurry and dry (solvent-free) processing of high-loading electrodes, checked from half cells to long-term full cells.
